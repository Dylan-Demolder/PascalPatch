Wavedash Trainer breaks down every wavedash you do and tells you the one thing to change.

After each wavedash, a line above your damage meter gives the verdict ("Wavedash: frame 1, 17 deg, 100% length", "Too early: L/R 2 frames before the jump", "Stick too flat: the air dodge went sideways") and a tip. The panel in the top right shows:

- A **frame strip**: your jumpsquat (grey, with a red mark where you pressed L/R too early), the frames you spent in the air before the air dodge (orange), the air dodge itself, and the 10 frames of landing lag (blue).
- A **stick gauge**: the red band is the part of the stick the game reads as flat, the green lines are the shallowest angle that still wavedashes, and the yellow line is where your stick was.
- The **angle** and the **length**: how far this wavedash slid next to the longest your character can do, in percent and in units.
- The **gap**: frames between the end of one wavedash's landing lag and the next jump, when you chain them.
- Your last 16 attempts as coloured squares, and a count of perfect and clean ones. Press **F12** to reset the count.

What counts as perfect comes from the game itself:

- L/R pressed during jumpsquat does nothing. The earliest air dodge is on the frame jumpsquat ends, which goes straight from jumpsquat to the landing. That is "frame 1". Each frame later loses speed and length.
- Stick heights up to 0.28 read as zero, so a stick that is too flat air dodges sideways and never lands. The shallowest angle that works is about 17 degrees below horizontal, and it gives the longest slide.
- Because the air dodge is the same for everyone, the best wavedash lands at the same speed for every character; how far it slides depends on your character's traction (Luigi slides far, Peach barely moves).

Coach one port, or every human player at once. Out-of-shield wavedashes are labelled as such; straight-down ones count as wavedashes in place. The plugin only reads game memory; it changes nothing.
